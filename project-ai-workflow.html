The Challenge

Most design teams treat AI tools as a curiosity — something to experiment with, not to build workflows around. When I joined SkinnyCoffee as Creative Lead, the team was producing good work, but slowly. Ideation was the bottleneck. Creative concepting and visual exploration were consuming time that should have been spent on execution quality.

The goal: integrate AI tools into the production workflow in a structured way — not to replace design thinking, but to compress the time between brief and first concept. Then train the team to use them confidently and consistently.

The AI Workflow System

Phase 1 — Brief Deconstruction with ChatGPT-4o

Every brief goes through a structured ChatGPT-4o prompt sequence: extract the core business goal, define the target audience in precise terms, generate 5 visual direction hypotheses, and identify the top 2 that would most likely perform. This replaces a 90-minute team ideation session with a focused 15-minute process.

Phase 2 — Visual Exploration with Midjourney

Use the 2 chosen directions as Midjourney prompts to generate 8–12 visual concepts before opening Illustrator or Photoshop. This gives the team and client a concrete reference point early, reducing revision loops significantly.

Phase 3 — Execution in Adobe Suite / Figma

With a direction locked, execution happens in the traditional tools. AI-generated concepts are never final deliverables — they're starting points. The designer's craft is in the execution, the refinement, and the on-brand adaptation.

Phase 4 — Brand QA Gate

Every asset passes through a brand QA checklist before delivery. AI tools can drift off-brand; the QA gate catches that before it reaches the client. This step was non-negotiable when introducing AI into the workflow.

Training the Team

Introducing AI tools to junior designers required a careful approach. The risk wasn't that they'd use them badly — it was that they'd use them as a crutch and skip the strategic thinking that makes a brief worth solving.

I designed a two-week onboarding programme: first week was prompt engineering fundamentals and tool-specific sessions. Second week was supervised practice on live briefs, with critique sessions focused on understanding when AI output needed to be pushed further before acceptance.
